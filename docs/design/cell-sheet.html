Version 4.6 · generic grid drawing on catalogue cards

Nock cell sheet

Every mark a letter or clue cell can carry, in the « Le Quotidien » palette. Version 3.3 shows each player on a cell as a coloured triangle in one corner, four at most, with no text and no outline, at 30 % of the cell side. Who found a letter is shown by an « Auteurs » mode that colours each letter in its finder's colour (switch below, or Menu on the phone). The revealed-letter mark moves to a small notch at the middle of the bottom edge, so all four corners stay free for players and no arrow ever meets it.

RecapWhere the design stands

Look

  • « Le Quotidien »: white, navy #1B2F55, blue-grey clue cells, after Le Figaro's phone view.
  • Fonts: Source Sans 3 for the interface and letters, IBM Plex Sans Condensed for clues.
  • Icons: the thin outline icons of this sheet. When one is missing, Font Awesome Free, outline (« regular ») style first, served by Nock itself. Grid arrows stay drawn for Nock.
  • Bold on grey means locked (checked right, or revealed). Pencil is italic grey. Wrong is struck through.
  • New: other players are a coloured triangle in a corner, four per cell at most. No dashed border, no shapes.
  • Other players: plain colour triangles, never any name or initials, no outline, 30 % of the cell side.
  • New: no author band. An « Auteurs » mode colours each letter in a darker ink of its finder's colour.
  • New: a revealed letter has an orange-red notch at the middle of the bottom edge, pointing up.

Screen

  • The grid comes first. Chat, players, progress and settings open on demand.
  • Phone and upright tablet: top bar, grid, navy clue bar with ⚑, keyboard. Panels as a bottom sheet.
  • Computer and landscape tablet: a side column, open by default; the grid fits what is left.
  • Phone held sideways: column only while the keyboard is hidden.
  • Top bar: timer, Annuler, Aide, Salon, Clavier, Menu.

Behaviour

  • Keyboard: ✎ where Shift would be (keys go italic grey when on), ⇄, ⌫. « Clavier » hides it.
  • « Aide » groups check and reveal in one menu.
  • One stream for chat and activity, in the Salon.
  • 3-second notes over the grid for messages, flags, reveals, checks, arrivals.

Still open

  • Settled: tint on other players' words kept, switchable (« Mots des autres »); Auteurs switchable, off by default; Accessibilité mode (icons) switchable, off by default; corners by arrival; a closed column stays closed; no long press on ⌫; grid lines #9AA3B2.
  • Deferred, high: the revealed notch is smaller than spec 024 allows.
  • Settled: fonts as npm dependencies (@fontsource, self-hosted); letters of a player who has left are drawn in ink.
  • Open: notch in ink during a flash (applied here, to approve); catalogue thumbnails need clue positions from the API, or go.

0 · Colour standardsOne palette for the whole app

The grid's colours are the app's colours. Menus, buttons, sheets, the catalogue and every later screen take their colours from this list and from nowhere else. Each colour has one job; a colour is never borrowed for another job.

ColourNameJobNever forOn white

The 8 player colours, imposed

Given in join order: the first player gets colour 1, and so on. They were chosen by a search, not by eye, under four rules: every colour reads at 3:1 or more as a plain triangle even on my selected blue cell; every Auteurs « ink » reads at 4.5:1 there; no blue, so no player looks like my own selection; and the colours stay apart for normal vision and for the three kinds of colour blindness (protan, deutan, tritan, simulated). The order puts the most distinct colours first, because most rooms have two to four players.

#TriangleNameOn whiteOn my selected cellAuteurs inkInk on selected

How far apart they stay (colour difference ΔE 2000, worst pair over the four kinds of vision, ink included; 10 is clearly visible on small marks): players 1 to 4, at least 10.9; all 8, at least 7.1 (Violet and Magenta for protan vision, Orange and Vert for deutan). The old palette fell to 0.9. Eight colours dark enough to need no outline cannot all stay at 10 apart for every kind of colour blindness; with 5 players or more, the player list remains the reference.

Rules

  • Navy is the app's own colour: bars, icons, primary buttons, my selection, focus.
  • Red means « look here »: wrong letters, the ⚑ flag, unread badges, errors.
  • Orange-red means « revealed », and nothing else.
  • Green means « done »: the finished grid, a passed check in the stream.
  • Player colours only mean a player: triangles, word tints, dots, Auteurs ink. Never a button or a state.

Contrast

  • Text and icons: 4.5:1 at least, disabled buttons included.
  • Marks, borders that carry meaning, focus: 3:1 at least.
  • Pure black and pure grey are not used: neutrals lean slightly blue, toward the navy.
  • No dark mode for now; every colour is defined once, as a named colour, so one can be added later.

1 · Players on a cellOne corner each, four at most

Players take the corners in the order they arrive on the cell: top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left. A fifth player on the same cell is not drawn; the player list still says where everyone is. Each triangle is plain colour, with no name and no outline; its side is 30 % of the cell side (6 px at least). Who is who is in the player list.

Accessibility. The corner is colour only, which spec 035 (AC-6) does not allow today. With the imposed colours, every triangle reaches 3:1 without an outline, even on my selected cell. The player list in the Salon says where each player is, which spec 035 would need to accept as the non-colour sign.

2 · Auteurs modeLetters in the colour of whoever found them

A switch, off by default, in the Menu (and in the bar above for this page). When it is on, each letter takes its finder's colour. Some player colours are too light for text, so each one gets an « ink », darkened only as far as needed to read at 4.5:1 on my selected cell, the darkest background a letter can have. Revealed letters have no finder and stay ink. A player who has left: their letters go back to ink. Pencil letters keep their italics, in their finder's colour.

Not by colour alone. With the mode on, a small legend under the grid names each colour. The clue bar stays for the clue only. The inks come from the imposed player colours (section 0) and were part of the same colour-blindness check.

3 · Clue cellsClues and their four arrows

The arrow sits in the letter cell next to the clue, as in printed grids. Two clues share a cell with a thin rule between them. A flagged clue (« Je bloque ») gets ⚑ in its top-left corner, the one corner no arrow uses.

4 · Letter cellsOne state at a time, at 56 px

Bold always means locked: a normal letter is weight 500, a locked one 700. A letter is locked when a check finds it right or when a reveal fills it in. Colour is never the only sign: pencil is italic, wrong is struck through, revealed has an orange-red notch at the bottom.

5 · Selection and other playersMe, my word, and the others

My cell keeps the filled blue and the solid navy border; it is the only cell with a border. Other players are a corner triangle and, if kept, a light tint of their colour on their word.

6 · Worst casesWhere marks could collide

Blue against blue: solved. The imposed palette has no blue, so no other player's word can look like my own selected word.

6b · Arrows against other marksEvery overlap, arrow by arrow

Each row is one arrow, drawn from its clue cell into a 64 px letter cell. Each column adds marks to that letter cell: a player in each corner, four players, the red revealed notch, and the selected cell. Drawing order, top first: my selection border, then the direction arrows (with a thin white halo), the orange-red notch, and the player triangles.

9 · Computer and tabletThe room with its side column

Landscape windows keep the column open by default. The grid takes all the height left; the clue bar sits under it, as wide as the grid. The grid below is a made-up 13 × 17 frame to show the layout at full size, not a playable grid.

Computer, 1280 × 800. Top bar with words beside the icons; « Salon » is pressed because the column is open. No on-screen keyboard. Cells are about 40 px, so player triangles stay readable.

Tablet held sideways, 1024 × 768. The keyboard sits under the grid only, so the column keeps its full height for the chat. Cells drop to about 29 px; « Clavier » hides the keyboard and gives them the space back. Finding: at 29 px, two-clue cells no longer hold their text (see the top rows): on a tablet with the keyboard shown, players will pinch to zoom, as spec 006 already allows, or hide the keyboard.

10 · CatalogueThe list of grids

Same white page, navy actions, blue-grey details. Each grid is a card: a small drawing of its shape, its number and date, force, size and word count, its progress, and « Jouer ». Force is written and drawn as notches, so it never relies on the drawing alone.

11 · OverlaysVote, end of grid, recap

Things that ask for an answer or close the game come over the grid without moving it. The vote is a banner over the top of the grid; the end of the grid is a band under the clue bar; the recap opens only when asked, as a sheet on the phone and a dialog on the computer.

Spec changes these need. « Voir le bilan » moves from the toolbar (spec 028 puts it after the chat button) into the end-of-grid band. The vote banner keeps spec 025's place, over the top of the grid.
